How the analytical workflow is organised

1. Start with market context

Review the instrument, trading session, volatility conditions and higher-timeframe direction before focusing on a lower-timeframe setup. A single candle or indicator should not decide a trade.

2. Build a confluence view

Bring structure, liquidity behaviour, volume spread and related-market observations together. Conflicting evidence should be treated as uncertainty, not forced into a trade idea.

3. Define risk before action

Document the thesis, invalidation level, stop distance, position size and maximum acceptable loss. Reassess the plan when market conditions change.

Who may find this framework useful?

The framework is intended for traders who want to organise chart research across forex, gold and other market instruments. Beginners should first build a foundation in market terminology, order types and risk management through the free learning centre. Experienced users may compare the framework with their own research process and platform workflow on the platform overview.

SMC and ICT terminology describes analytical concepts, not a promise that institutions or market participants will behave in a predictable way. VSA observations depend on the quality and meaning of the volume data available for a particular instrument and provider.

Risk planning remains essential

AI output can be incomplete, delayed or incorrect. Analysis is decision support, not a guaranteed signal, verified win rate or promise of returns. Always define position size, invalidation, exposure and maximum loss before trading.
